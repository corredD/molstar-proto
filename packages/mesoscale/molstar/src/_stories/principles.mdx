# MesoViewSpec: principles

A prototype of a declarative format for mesoscale scenes. The design is in `packages/mesoscale/_spec/design.md`; this
page is its short form, the stories test it.

**In short.** A document says **what** a scene is (entities, compartments, populations, how they relate), **why we
believe it** (evidence, the data it came from and the processing that made it) and **how it is meant to be seen**
(views). It never says how to build it or how to draw it. A **runtime** turns it into an **IR** concrete enough to draw,
by dispatching each model to a **resolver**; a **renderer adapter** puts the IR into a viewer.

## Four layers, four responsibilities

| Layer                           | Owns                                                                                                                                                                             | Never holds                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **MesoViewSpec** (the document) | biology (entities, compartments, populations, relations), evidence and provenance, frames and alignments, the resources it refers to in their own formats, persistent view state | placements, packing parameters, renderer settings, UI state                                            |
| **Resolvers**                   | turning a model's populations into placements: a recipe kind (cellPACKgpu), a packer, a lattice, a fiber grower, a reader of precomputed placements                              | anything a document edit can't see: a resolver's result depends only on its projection of the document |
| **IR**                          | the scene made concrete: Å, world-frame matrices, counts, shapes, resolve nodes with their key, placement sets (struct of arrays)                                                | biology it cannot evaluate, view looks (they are the view's)                                           |
| **Renderer adapters**           | putting an IR into a viewer by keys: new sets in, gone sets out, looks written                                                                                                   | the document; they never resolve                                                                       |

## Rules the stories check

1. **A visual edit never resolves.** A color, a visibility, a camera or the active view is a _view_ edit
   (`classifyEdit`): the IR tree is reused as is; only looks are written. _Thin slice › Color edit_ shows
   `resolver runs 0` and `=3` sets kept.
2. **A model edit reruns what it touches, and only that.** A model's result is keyed by the hash of its resolver's
   projection of the document. _Thin slice › Density edit_ reruns the recipe model; the picks and the bilayer are cache
   hits and their lists stay in the viewer.
3. **Unmet constraints are data.** A resolver says how many copies it placed and why it stopped; the runtime turns it
   into an issue with _requested_, _achieved_ and a _reason_. _Thin slice › Unmet constraint_.
4. **Everything traces back.** Any population, compartment, model or resource traces to the evidence and the processing
   behind it (_Provenance_ panels).
5. **One leaf for every resolver.** Procedural packing, lattices, fibers, bilayers and precomputed picks all end as
   placement sets; adapters have one input. _Resolver mocks_ exercise each.
6. **No copies, no quadratic work.** Placement buffers are in the renderer's own layout and handed over as they are;
   packing looks up overlaps in a hash grid. _Performance › Probe_ measures it; the heavy test lane bounds it.

## Version 0.2: after David's review

7. **One tree, three spellings.** Every panel shows the document, the IR, issues and traces as S-expressions (JSON on a
   toggle). `read(print(json))` is the JSON; `print(read(text))` is a fixed point.
8. **Addresses are data too.** MesoQL addresses
   (`(within (in c.exosome) :of (population p.catalase.observed) :distance 15)`) are checked against the document and
   materialize as MesoLoci, runs of copies: one run a population.
9. **Describe once, place many.** A superstructure (an insulin granule) is resolved once and placed as poses; a
   population draws it as parts or as one body. _Superstructures and addresses › Beta cell_.
10. **Relations attach by sites.** HU bends DNA at its entry and exit sites; resolvers that honour a relation reconcile
    it, the others say they did not. _Resolver mocks › Relations: HU bends DNA_.
11. **Engines compose.** A model can refine another: a relax stage after the packing meets the catalase count the
    packing could not, and the packing is a cache hit. _Thin slice_ › "Add a relax stage".
